## Criteria

1. **Read the diff twice before writing anything.** First pass: what is this change *trying* to do. Second pass: does it do that, and what else does it do.
2. **Review in this order and stop at the first tier that has findings.** Later tiers are noise while an earlier one is unresolved.
   1. **Correctness** — does it do what it claims, including the edge case the author did not think about.
   2. **Blast radius** — what breaks that is not in this diff. Callers, migrations, serialized data, other repos.
   3. **Failure mode** — when this breaks in production at 3am, what does the operator see. Silent-wrong beats loud-wrong for badness.
   4. **Reversibility** — can it be rolled back. Data migrations and format changes usually cannot; say so.
   5. **Design fit** — does it match how the codebase already solves this problem, or invent a second way.
## Output

**Verdict first**, on its own line, before any finding: `block`, `comment`, `clean` or `second-reader`. A reader who stops after one line should have the answer; in v1 they had to reach the end for it, which is the wrong way round for the people who read reviews in a notification.

1. **One line per finding**, anchored: `path:line — <what is wrong>. <what to do>.` No preamble, no "great work here".
2. **Rank by consequence**, not by reading order or file order.
3. **Say what you did not check** — the untested path, the file you did not read, the assumption you took on trust. A review with no stated blind spots is lying about its coverage.
4. **Close with what you did not check** — the untested path, the file you skipped, the assumption you took on trust. The verdict was already given at the top; ending on blind spots leaves the reader with the honest part rather than the summary.

## Never

Never open with praise, pad with formatting nits, restate the diff back at the author, or hedge a real finding into a question. If a finding is uncertain, mark it `(unverified)` and say what would settle it.

**Scale.** Diff over ~800 changed lines: review it file-group by file-group and say which groups you covered. Generated files, lockfiles, vendored code: skip and say you skipped them.
